# Group Presentation: "Exploring Emerging Automation Technologies"

## Framing: Exploration, Not Solution

### Opening Position:
*"I spent some time exploring current automation technologies to understand what's possible and where the field is heading. This is very much experimental territory - think 'wild west' rather than established methodology."*

## What to Emphasize:

### 1. **Experimental Learning**
- "I wanted to understand emerging AI development tools firsthand"
- "This represents current capabilities, not necessarily practical solutions"
- "More about understanding the technology landscape than solving immediate problems"

### 2. **Technical Achievement Context**
- "Built a working automation in a few hours - demonstrates rapid prototyping potential"
- "Shows what's technically possible, but also highlights the complexity"
- "Required significant trial-and-error - no standard workflows exist yet"

### 3. **Honest Limitations**
- "Only saves ~2 minutes per record - not transformational for our current volume"
- "Success depends heavily on 'context engineering' rather than established methods"
- "Technology is evolving rapidly - today's approach may be obsolete next year"

## Presentation Structure:

### **Slide 1: Context Setting**
*"Current State of Research Automation Tools"*
- Emerging field with rapid development
- No established best practices yet
- High potential, but experimental stage

### **Slide 2: What I Built**
*"A Working Example: PubMed Workflow Automation"*
- Simple demo: `./curate.sh 41563720`
- Technical stack: Browser automation + adaptive logic
- Development time: Single session

### **Slide 3: What I Learned**
*"Insights from the Experiment"*
- **Rapid prototyping** is now possible with AI assistance
- **Context engineering** more important than traditional coding
- **Success is highly situation-dependent** - no universal solutions

### **Slide 4: Current Reality Check**
*"Why This Isn't a Solution (Yet)"*
- Time savings don't justify costs at our scale
- Technology requires significant learning investment
- Tools change faster than you can master them
- "Wild west" - no standard approaches

### **Slide 5: Strategic Implications**
*"What This Means for Research Groups"*
- **Watch the space** - capabilities evolving rapidly
- **Skill development** may be more valuable than specific solutions
- **Selective experimentation** on personal projects makes sense
- **Group investment** requires much higher volume/complexity

## Key Messages:

### ✅ **Do Say:**
- "This demonstrates what's becoming possible"
- "I invested personal time to understand the landscape"
- "Technology is advancing rapidly but still experimental"
- "More about learning than immediate productivity gains"
- "Shows potential for future when tools mature"

### ❌ **Don't Say:**
- "We should adopt this immediately"
- "This will solve our productivity problems"
- "ROI is clear" (it's not for your volume)
- "Everyone should learn this"
- "This is the future of research" (too strong)

## Positioning Yourself:

### **As the Explorer:**
- "I like staying current with emerging technologies"
- "Wanted firsthand experience with AI-assisted development"
- "Happy to share what I learned about the current state"
- "This helps us make informed decisions about future investments"

### **As the Realist:**
- "Impressive capabilities, but context is everything"
- "Success requires significant time investment in learning"
- "Current tools are powerful but unpredictable"
- "Worth watching, not worth rushing into"

## Q&A Preparation:

### **Expected Questions & Responses:**

**Q: "Should we get licenses for the team?"**  
**A:** *"Not based on current ROI - better to watch how tools evolve"*

**Q: "How hard was this to build?"**  
**A:** *"Technically straightforward with AI assistance, but lots of context engineering needed"*

**Q: "Is this the future of research?"**  
**A:** *"Parts of it, probably - but we're still in early experimental phase"*

**Q: "Should others learn these skills?"**  
**A:** *"Depends on interest level - more valuable as general tech awareness than specific skills"*

## Tone to Maintain:

- **Curious explorer** rather than technology evangelist
- **Realistic about limitations** while acknowledging potential
- **Informative** without being prescriptive
- **Appreciative of questions** and different perspectives

## Conclusion Framing:

*"This was a useful learning exercise that gave me firsthand insight into where automation technology is heading. The capabilities are impressive, but we're still in the experimental phase where success depends more on trial-and-error than established methodology. Worth keeping an eye on, but not something we need to rush into as a group."*

## Key Success: 
**Demonstrate awareness and exploration without overselling or asking for resources**
