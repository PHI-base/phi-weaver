# Sophie Eisele - PhD Project Development Session

**Date**: 2026-04-29

**Context**: PhD project scoping for biocuration AI system research
**Earmarked for**: **Sophie Eisele - PhD Project - 2026**

## Session Context

Martin (PHI-base biocurator and database manager) and Claude collaboratively developed a 6-module biocuration AI system. Discussion focused on PhD project potential, academic concerns about overlapping with existing research, and reframing the research approach as design space exploration.

## Discussion Topics

### 1. PhD Project Potential Assessment

**Initial Enthusiasm**: Identified 4 potential PhD projects based on the 6-module biocuration system:

#### **Potential PhD Areas Identified**:
1. **Automated Quality Assessment & Learning Systems** (Module 5 focus)
   - Machine learning for biocuration quality prediction
   - Confidence scoring algorithms
   - Adaptive learning from curator feedback

2. **Human-AI Collaborative Biocuration Methodologies**
   - Novel collaboration frameworks and workflow optimization
   - Empirical studies of human-AI collaboration effectiveness
   - Comparative analysis vs traditional methods

3. **Automated Ontology Integration & Semantic Mapping** (Modules 3 & 6)
   - Multi-ontology reconciliation and automated mapping
   - Cross-ontology consistency algorithms
   - API integration architectures

4. **Advanced Biomedical Document Processing** (Modules 1 & 2)
   - Multi-modal analysis of scientific literature
   - Domain-specific language models for biology
   - Figure-text integration for entity recognition

### 2. Conservative Assessment

**Academic and Technical Risks Identified**:

#### **Technical Challenges**:
- **Limited training data**: PHI-base scope relatively narrow
- **Integration complexity**: API dependencies, ontology evolution
- **Evaluation difficulty**: Subjective quality assessment challenges
- **Current automation estimate** (69%) may be optimistic

#### **Academic Risks**:
- **Publication challenges**: Falls between CS and Biology disciplines  
- **Niche field**: Small biocuration community (~hundreds of curators globally)
- **"Good enough" problem**: Existing manual methods work adequately
- **Resource requirements**: Need constant domain expert input

#### **Competitive Threats**:
- **Existing solutions**: Manual curation still preferred for quality
- **Large language models**: GPT-4/Claude may solve problems directly
- **Commercial development**: Elsevier, Springer competing systems

### 3. Colleague's Concern: Impinging on Current PhD Students

**Valid Worry Acknowledged**:
- Risk of creating competition vs collaboration with existing PhD students
- Potential duplication of efforts in limited research space
- Need to survey existing work before pursuing projects
- Importance of building on rather than competing with current research

**Constructive Response Strategy**:
- **Research due diligence**: Literature review, conference networking
- **Collaboration emphasis**: "How can we help?" vs "Here's our competing approach"
- **Differentiation focus**: Underexplored modules, domain specificity
- **Ethical practice**: Cite existing work, propose partnerships

### 4. Key Reframing: Design Space Exploration

**Brilliant Insight**: Our 6-module system is **one point in a design space**, not the optimal solution.

#### **Transformed Research Question**:
**"What is the space of possible interactive biocurator-AI systems, and how do we systematically explore it?"**

#### **PhD Thesis Concept**: 
**"Design Space Exploration of Human-AI Collaborative Biocuration Systems"**

**Research Methodology**:
- **Multi-Curator Design Study**: Pair different curators with AI independently
- **Comparative Analysis**: Compare resulting architectures and effectiveness  
- **Pattern Extraction**: Identify invariant principles vs variable design choices
- **Framework Development**: Create evaluation methods for collaborative systems

**Academic Strengths**:
- **Fundamental vs Applied Research**: Studies collaboration methodology, not just implementation
- **Generalizability**: Applicable beyond biocuration to any expert-AI collaboration
- **Research Novelty**: Creates new research area rather than solving single problem
- **Collaboration-Friendly**: Includes existing work as valuable data points
